Case study

fccid.info: a public government database turned into a search site

Almost every wireless device sold in the US carries an FCC ID, and its certification records sit in a public FCC database that is hard to search. I built fccid.info on top of that data: one page per device and per manufacturer, generated from the records, with the domain matching exactly what people type into Google.

The lesson I bring to every project: a domain or subfolder that names the topic, a clean page structure, and data people actually want gets pages indexed fast, without a long wait for authority.

FAQ

Questions people ask before starting

Won't Google treat thousands of generated pages as spam?

Google penalizes pages that exist only to catch searches without helping anyone. The pages I build are only worth publishing when each one carries real, specific data that answers its search. If your data can't do that for a page type, I'll tell you not to build it.

What kind of data do I need?

Structured records with something unique per row: products, locations, companies, integrations, specs, prices, reviews or statistics. A database export, a spreadsheet, an API, or a public dataset we enrich with your expertise all work.

Do the pages live on my site?

Usually yes, in a subfolder (yoursite.com/devices/…) so they build on your domain's authority. In some cases a separate exact-match domain is the better choice; we decide that in the audit.

How long until I see traffic?

Indexing typically starts within weeks, and traffic grows over the following months as pages earn rankings. It depends on your domain, competition and how much search demand the data covers. The audit gives you a realistic estimate before you commit.
